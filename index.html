01 · Abstract

Make uncertainty a first-class output.

JevRL is a minimal research interface for software that needs decisions it can inspect, score, and safely decline.

Abstract. Language models are often asked to produce a single answer even when a downstream system needs a probability, a score, or an explicit abstention signal. JevRL turns a frozen small language model into three typed outputs: a categorical choice, a scalar score, and a null/abstention probability. Two independent samples are rewarded for matching a latent target while duplicate answers are penalized, yielding a quadratic proper objective in expectation. On a one-seed synthetic pilot with Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, JevRL reaches 0.611 accuracy, 0.093 true-target Brier score, and 0.040 expected calibration error.

Choice, Score, and Noul are separate values that downstream code can consume directly.

A two-sample reward discourages brittle certainty and keeps probability mass meaningful.

02 · Method

A typed head around a frozen language model.

The pilot isolates the interface idea: keep the language model fixed and learn only the small decision head.

Figure 1. State → frozen Qwen2.5-0.5B → typed head → proper two-sample reward.

Given a state and candidate set, the frozen backbone produces a hidden representation. The head emits a categorical distribution over candidates, a bounded scalar, and a null probability.

R(A, B, y) = 1[A = y] + 1[B = y] − 1[A = B]

In the categorical setting, the expected reward is ||q||² − ||p − q||². Maximizing it is equivalent to minimizing a squared probability error up to a constant.

ChoiceA probability distribution over candidates.
ScoreA bounded confidence or urgency value.
NoulAn explicit probability of abstention.

05 · Scope

What this pilot does and does not show.

Limitations

  • Synthetic benchmark and one random seed.
  • Only the compact head is trained; the backbone stays frozen.
  • Noul is exposed but not yet trained with a selective-prediction dataset.
  • No claim of reproducing an external Jev system or of production readiness.

Future work

  • Evaluate real decision benchmarks with soft labels and distribution shift.
  • Compare frozen, adapter-tuned, and fully tuned language models.
  • Train abstention against selective risk and downstream escalation cost.
  • Measure whether typed outputs improve tool routing and agent reliability.
